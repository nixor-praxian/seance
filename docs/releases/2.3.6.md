# 2.3.6: the watcher stops wearing down WindowServer

## What you'll notice

Mostly nothing, and that's the point. After about a week of uptime, macOS
could kill WindowServer. That logs you out and relaunches every app. The
seance watcher was a big part of why.

To spot a display being plugged in or removed, the watcher started a new
`osascript` about every 2.4 seconds and asked it for the screen list. That's
about 36,000 processes a day. Every one of them opens a connection to
WindowServer, and WindowServer doesn't give back everything when the
connection closes. It keeps a couple of Mach ports each time. After a week
that adds up to its limit, and the kernel kills it.

Now the watcher starts one small helper and keeps it for as long as it runs.
The helper checks the displays once a second inside its own process and
tells the watcher when something changes. In normal use the watcher starts
no other WindowServer clients: painting goes straight to each pane's TTY,
and finding panes uses `ps` and `lsof`.

There's also a new line in `~/.config/seance/watcher.log` once an hour: how
many `osascript` runs the watcher started, how many failed, their average
time, and whether the display helper is up. If this ever creeps back, that
line is where it shows.

## Do I need to do anything?

Restart the watcher so it picks up the new build:

```bash
launchctl kickstart -k gui/$(id -u)/com.seance.watcher
```

## Under the hood

The leak is per client process, not per call, and seance's scripts aren't
doing anything wrong. Measured on macOS 27.2, as WindowServer's port count
after a batch of runs, 15 to 60 seconds after the last one exited:

| 200 runs of | ports kept |
|---|---|
| `osascript -e 'return 1'` | +38 (noise) |
| bare JXA | -26 (noise) |
| JXA reading `NSScreen.screens` | +455 |
| JXA calling `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo` | +289 |
| AppleScript `tell application "System Events"` | +369 |

The watcher's old screen read (AppKit plus the window list, in one process)
kept 371 ports over 300 runs, about 1.24 each. One process polling
CoreGraphics 120 times kept none (-41). At 1.24 per run, 7.5 days of polling
every 2.4 seconds is about 270,000 runs and 334,000 ports. That's past the
roughly 305,000 that WindowServer was allowed.

The helper is a long-lived JXA script that reads `CGGetActiveDisplayList` and
`CGDisplayBounds` with each display's UUID, the same signature the watcher
already keys arrangements on. It prints the signature when it changes and an
empty heartbeat line otherwise. The watcher still re-reads the full geometry
through `listScreens()` before acting, so nothing downstream changed. It
also re-reads every five minutes as a safety net. If the helper exits, the
watcher logs why and restarts it after 5 s, 15 s, 1 min, then 5 min. If it
goes 30 s without a heartbeat, the watcher kills it and restarts it. While
it's down, the watcher reads once a minute instead of every pass. The
heartbeat also means the helper dies by itself within a second if the
watcher is killed, so nothing gets orphaned.

Something that looked like evidence but wasn't: WindowServer logs
`_CGXPackagesSetWindowConstraints: Invalid window` at a high rate. It kept
going at the same rate (661 vs 681 in 90 s) with the watcher stopped and
Ghostty closed, so another process is producing it. It doesn't say who's
leaking ports.
